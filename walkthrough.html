What the office gets from one affection plan

A walkthrough of M1 on a fictional Dubai villa plot, plus the 100 m context on a real public plot, for Amir Hossein's review. Step 1 of 10

Input: the affection plan, or coordinates, or a plot number

The office uploads the plan as PDF or JPEG. The engine first reads the printed issuer mark, never the community name, because a Nakheel community can still be routed to Trakhees. If the mark is unclear it asks; it never guesses.

Step 2 of 10

Extraction: every field with its status, confidence and source location

The ladder is text layer first, then OCR, then at most two vision-model passes only for fields still missing or conflicting. A model may read a value; only a person confirms it. Unread stays "not read". Values the issuer does not print read "not printed", never a guess.

Step 4 of 10

Site record: corners in Dubai Local TM, WGS 84 derived, area checked

Corners come from the issuer's DLTM numbers, never from a basemap click. WGS 84 values are derived and marked so. The computed area is compared with the printed area; a gap over 0.5% is flagged for the office.

Step 5 of 10

Context: the 100 m around the plot, its centre, the streets, the neighbours and the entrances

This is the view Amir Hossein asked for: the plot with its exact coordinates, its centre and the circle around it, which streets border it, what stands next to it, and where the entrance is. The fictional plot has no real surroundings, so this step uses real public plots (mosque buildings) in three areas with open data: OpenStreetMap for streets and buildings, and Dubai Municipality's open Entrances layer for the official entrance points with their Makani numbers and DLTM coordinates. Switch between Al Barsha Second, where OSM is nearly complete, and two first-segment areas, where it is thin, to see what the engine can and cannot say.

Step 6 of 10

Envelope: setback per edge, buildable area, floors and height datums

Each edge gets its own setback from the plan. The buildable polygon is the mitred inward offset [assumption until Amir Hossein confirms how corners are measured]. Coverage and GFA limits are only computed where the issuer sets them; on this DM private-villa plan they read "not assessed".

Direct-sun hours count the sun above the horizon on the facade's outward side in 10-minute steps; they ignore shading by neighbours (the picture shows it). Heights: DM's Buildings Summary holds floor counts and heights but no coordinates (R03-g1-H04), so untagged neighbours use the assumed height above until DM GIS layers are available; OSM tags levels on very few villas. Storey height is a design input, not a regulation; the DBC 16 m cap is checked in Step 8.
Step 8 of 10

Rule trace: one line per constraint, with its source and status

Six outcomes exist: limit_set, pass, fail, not_assessed, not_applicable, conflict. "Compliant" is never one of them. A rule with an input that is not confirmed reads not_assessed. Conflicts show both values and are never averaged.

"unverified" rows are placeholders that the pack author could not source; they never produce a limit.
